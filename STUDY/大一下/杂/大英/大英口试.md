### 13. Game theory

- **Definition**：Game theory is a branch of mathematics that studies strategic interactions among rational decision-makers. It analyzes how individuals, firms, or countries make decisions in situations where the outcome depends not only on their own actions but also on the actions of others.
- **Key concepts**：
    - **Players**：The individuals or entities making decisions in the game.
    - **Strategies**：The options or courses of action available to the players.
    - **Payoffs**：The rewards or outcomes that each player receives depending on the combination of strategies chosen by all players.
    - **Nash equilibrium**：A situation where each player's strategy is optimal given the strategies of the other players. In other words, no player can improve their payoff by unilaterally changing their strategy.


### 13. Game theory

- **Definition and Basics**：Game theory, also known as decision theory or game strategy theory, is a mathematical theory that studies how decision-makers make strategic choices in competitive situations. It originated from the mathematical study of games such as gambling, chess, and poker. The basic elements include players (participants who make decisions), strategies (plans to achieve goals), payoffs (gains or losses), game form (mathematical model), and Nash equilibrium (a state where all players maximize their payoffs). For example, in the Prisoner's Dilemma, two criminals face different sentences based on whether they cooperate or betray each other, showing a conflict between individual and collective rationality1.
- **Applications**：It is widely used in economics, such as analyzing the strategic choices of enterprises in a competitive market, negotiation strategies in international trade, and decision-making in political games. In business, it can be applied to pricing strategies, market entry and exit, cooperation and alliance, supply chain management, and advertising and marketing strategies14.

### 15. Do you agree that there's a special relationship between sounds and memories

- **Agree**：Sounds can strongly evoke memories. For example, a particular song may remind you of a specific period in your life, like your high school days or a memorable trip. The sound of an old record, the laughter of an old friend, or the melody of a lullaby from your childhood can all trigger vivid memories. This is because the auditory cortex in the brain is closely connected to the limbic system, which is involved in emotions and memory. When we experience a sound associated with a past event, it can activate the neural pathways related to that memory, bringing back feelings and details of the event.
- **Disagree**：Some may argue that while sounds can sometimes be associated with memories, it's not a universal or special relationship. Not all sounds have a strong impact on memory, and for some people, visual or tactile experiences may be more closely linked to memory. For example, a person might remember a place more vividly based on what they saw rather than what they heard. Also, in some cases, people may hear a sound frequently but not form any specific memories related to it if there is no significant event or emotion attached to it.

### 27. Should we explore extraterrestrial life when we know nothing about it

- **Reasons for exploration**：Firstly, human beings have an innate curiosity and a desire to explore the unknown. Exploring extraterrestrial life can satisfy this curiosity and expand our understanding of the universe. Secondly, it may bring new scientific discoveries and technological advancements. For example, the search for extraterrestrial life may drive the development of new telescopes, space probes, and communication technologies. Additionally, if we do find extraterrestrial life, it could have a profound impact on our understanding of life, evolution, and our place in the universe.
- **Concerns**：However, there are also concerns. We know very little about extraterrestrial life, and there may be risks involved. For example, the extraterrestrial life forms could be hostile or carry diseases that our bodies have no immunity to. Also, our exploration activities may interfere with the natural development of extraterrestrial life (if it exists) or cause environmental damage to other planets. Moreover, the cost of exploration is high, and the resources could be used for other important issues on Earth. A balanced approach is needed, perhaps starting with non-invasive exploration methods and careful consideration of the potential risks and benefits.
